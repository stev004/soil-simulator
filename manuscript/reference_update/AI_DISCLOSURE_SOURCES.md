# AI_DISCLOSURE_SOURCES

Authoritative journal policies and scholarly sources relevant to the manuscript's AI-assisted-development and computational-audit disclosure (Section 9). These support the statements that generative-AI use must be disclosed, that AI systems do not qualify as authors, that the human author retains responsibility, and that computational auditing does not replace human peer review.

**No journal-specific wording is selected here** — a target journal has not been chosen. Once it is, the author should adopt that publisher's current AI-disclosure wording (policies are revised periodically).

**Verification tiers:** entries with a DOI were verified against Crossref (high confidence). Publisher/organisation policy pages have no DOI; they were matched to their official URL and title via search but the live pages could not be fetched from this environment (medium confidence) — confirm current wording on the official page before use.

## Registry-verified (DOI, high confidence)

- **`nature_ground_rules_2023`** — Nature / Springer Nature (editorial) (2023). *Tools such as ChatGPT threaten transparent science; here are our ground rules for their use*. doi:10.1038/d41586-023-00191-1 https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-023-00191-1
  - *Type:* editorial
  - *Supports:* Nature editorial establishing that LLMs will not be accepted as authors and that any use must be documented; a human author bears responsibility for accuracy and integrity.

- **`science_not_an_author_2023`** — Science / AAAS (editorial, H. Holden Thorp) (2023). *ChatGPT is fun, but not an author*. doi:10.1126/science.adg7879 https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adg7879
  - *Type:* editorial
  - *Supports:* Science editorial stating AI text generators cannot be authors and that authors are accountable for all content, articulating the AAAS position on AI use.

- **`wame_recommendations_2023`** — World Association of Medical Editors (WAME) (2024). *Chatbots, generative AI, and scholarly manuscripts: WAME recommendations on chatbots and generative artificial intelligence in relation to scholarly publications*. doi:10.1080/03007995.2023.2286102 https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/03007995.2023.2286102
  - *Type:* guideline
  - *Supports:* WAME recommendations: chatbots/generative AI cannot be authors because they cannot be accountable; authors must disclose which AI tools were used and how, and remain responsible for the manuscript.

## Official policy pages (URL-verified, medium confidence — confirm current wording)

- **`icmje_ai_use_by_authors`** — International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE) (2024). *Recommendations for the Conduct, Reporting, Editing, and Publication of Scholarly Work in Medical Journals — Artificial Intelligence (AI)-Assisted Technology (Use of AI by authors)*. https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/artificial-intelligence/ai-use-by-authors.html
  - *Type:* guideline
  - *Supports:* Requires authors to disclose in the manuscript any use of AI-assisted technologies (naming the tool and how it was used) and to take full responsibility for the content, including AI-produced material.

- **`icmje_author_roles`** — International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE) (2024). *Defining the Role of Authors and Contributors*. https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/defining-the-role-of-authors-and-contributors.html
  - *Type:* guideline
  - *Supports:* Defines authorship criteria requiring accountability that a non-human system cannot meet, establishing that AI tools cannot be listed as authors and that a human author is accountable for the work.

- **`cope_authorship_ai_tools_2023`** — Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE) (2023). *Authorship and AI tools — COPE position statement*. https://publicationethics.org/guidance/cope-position/authorship-and-ai-tools
  - *Type:* position statement
  - *Supports:* States that AI tools cannot meet authorship requirements because they cannot take responsibility for the work, and that authors must be transparent about any AI use and remain accountable.

- **`springer_nature_ai_editorial_policy`** — Springer Nature / Nature Portfolio (2023). *Artificial Intelligence (AI) — Nature Portfolio editorial policies (Use of Large Language Models)*. https://www.nature.com/nature-portfolio/editorial-policies/ai
  - *Type:* journal policy
  - *Supports:* Springer Nature/Nature Portfolio policy: LLMs do not qualify for authorship; any use of an LLM must be documented in the methods or acknowledgements, with the human author responsible.

- **`elsevier_genai_journal_policy`** — Elsevier (2023). *Generative AI policies for journals*. https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/generative-ai-policies-for-journals
  - *Type:* journal policy
  - *Supports:* Elsevier author policy: generative-AI assistance in writing must be disclosed via a declaration; AI cannot be an author; authors take full responsibility and must review AI-assisted content.

- **`taylor_francis_ai_policy`** — Taylor & Francis (2023). *AI Policy (Taylor & Francis)*. https://taylorandfrancis.com/our-policies/ai-policy/
  - *Type:* journal policy
  - *Supports:* Taylor & Francis policy: AI tools cannot be listed as authors; authors must clearly disclose AI use and remain accountable for the integrity and validity of the content.

- **`wiley_research_integrity_ethics`** — Wiley (2023). *AI Guidelines (Wiley) — publisher best-practice guidance on AI use and authorship*. https://www.wiley.com/en-us/publish/book/resources/ai-guidelines/
  - *Type:* guideline
  - *Supports:* Wiley author policy: using AI tools for content generation is not in line with authorship criteria (per COPE), AI use must be acknowledged/documented, and the author is fully responsible for accuracy.

- **`science_editorial_policies_page`** — Science / AAAS (2023). *Science Journals: Editorial Policies (Artificial intelligence and generative AI section)*. https://www.science.org/content/page/science-journals-editorial-policies
  - *Type:* journal policy
  - *Supports:* Current AAAS editorial policy governing permitted uses and required disclosure of generative AI/LLMs across the Science family of journals, with AI barred from authorship.

- **`science_genai_policy_update`** — Science / AAAS (2023). *Change to policy on the use of generative AI and large language models*. https://www.science.org/content/blog-post/change-policy-use-generative-ai-and-large-language-models
  - *Type:* editorial
  - *Supports:* AAAS statement updating the Science policy to permit disclosed AI-assisted use while maintaining that AI cannot be an author and authors are accountable for all content.
